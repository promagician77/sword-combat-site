Reading the frame meter

Each cell is one frame at 60 Hz, newest on the right, the way fighting games show it in training mode. It answers the questions a combat designer asks when a move feels wrong. How long is the wind-up? How long can it hit? How long are you stuck afterwards? Did the hit-stop land? Did the dodge's invulnerability cover the slam?

  • Startup: wind-up, can't hit yet
  • Active: the blade can hit
  • Recovery: follow-through, open to punishment
  • Hit-stop: both fighters freeze on impact
  • Invulnerable: dodge frames that ignore hits
  • Dodge, outside the invulnerable frames
  • Hit reaction

What the measurements found

Fast swings pass through thin targets unless the blade is sampled by distance

A strike moves the sword tip more than a metre between frames. Checked once per frame, the blade missed 370 of the 456 thin targets it actually passed through. Four checks per frame still missed 146. The build spaces checks by how far the tip moved, at most 5 cm apart. That's about 30 checks in an active frame and none outside, and it missed 2. The whole simulation, hit checks included, costs 0.021 ms a frame.

Input read once per display frame changes the fight at 6 of 7 refresh rates

The same 40-second scripted fight was played at seven display rates. Reading input once per display frame, like polling in Update(), moved presses by up to 3 frames and changed the outcome: at 30 Hz the player landed 23 hits instead of 24; at 120 Hz they took 3 hits instead of 4. Applying each key event to the frame it happened in gave the identical fight, frame for frame, at 7 of 7 rates.

Blends remove the pose jumps, except dodge cancels, which snap on purpose

With hard cuts, 42 of 126 transitions turned a joint more than 45° in one frame. With blends, 3 did, and all of them were dodges cancelling out of an attack. The dodge blends in over 2 frames on purpose, because a dodge that eases in feels late. For scale, the strikes themselves turn a joint up to 102° a frame. Light 2 was keyed to start where Light 1 ends, so that chain barely moves even without a blend.

The fastest part of every swing lands inside its active frames

If the blade is still accelerating when the hit window closes, players see a hit that didn't count. For 4 of 4 attacks the tip's fastest frame falls inside the active window, and 46–62% of the tip's whole path is covered while the blade can hit. The rest is wind-up, follow-through and the return to guard.

Not measured

  • This is not Unity. It is JavaScript and three.js, so it runs in a browser. The Unity column above is a plan.
  • The mannequin animation is hand-keyed in code: no rig, no IK, no foot locking, no mocap. It shows timing and readability, not the polish bar for a shipped game.
  • One enemy with simple, seeded behaviour. No physics engine and no networking.
  • The thin-target "truth" is 256 blade samples per frame, not an exact swept-volume test.
  • Refresh rates were simulated by feeding the driver frame times; no real monitors were used.
  • Costs were measured in Node on a 2-core sandbox, not on target hardware. Screenshots used software WebGL.
